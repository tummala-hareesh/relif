## 2. CRA record-keeping rules (with citations)
- **Six-year rule.** Records and supporting documents are kept for six years after the end of the last tax year they relate to (calendar year for individuals). So a March 2026 purchase for tax year 2026 is kept until 31 Dec 2032. T4036: "keep your records for six years from the end of the tax year to which they relate"; do not send with the return, keep in case CRA asks. [S1, S2]
- **Late-filed return:** six years from the date you FILE that return (not year-end). [S2]
- **Objection/appeal:** keep until the latest of: resolution date, date for any further appeal has passed, six-year period passed. [S2]
- **Longer cases:** CRA page says records of long-term acquisitions and disposals of property (historical info affecting a sale) must be kept indefinitely; the page does not use "capital property" wording, but for a landlord this means purchase/closing statements, capital additions and CCA schedules for the property should be kept for as long as owned plus the 6 years after disposal (my reading; confirm). CRA can also set a different period by registered letter/personal demand. [S2]
- **Where:** at place of business or residence in Canada; records only accessible from Canada but stored abroad (e.g. a foreign cloud) do not count as kept in Canada without written permission. Relevant to any hosted-backup design. [S2]
- **What a supporting record is:** T4036 says support purchases and operating expenses with invoices, receipts, contracts or other supporting documents; without them an expense can be disallowed. [S1] I did NOT find a CRA page listing exact receipt fields for income-tax purposes (the 404 on RC4409 path; GST/HST ITC documentation rules have a vendor-info schedule but are not verified here). Design the schema to capture date, vendor, amount, tax, description, payment method, because those are what any auditor will look for (design choice, not a verified CRA field list). A card statement alone may be weaker support; the only CRA mention found is a travel-procedures exception (unverified for rentals).
- **Electronic / photographed receipts:** scanned images are acceptable if an accurate reproduction (no significant detail lost to resolution/tone/colour) and produced, controlled and kept to the national standard CAN/CGSB-72.34 (commercial scanning software for small scale should meet it); if so the image is the permanent record and paper may be destroyed; if you can't meet the standard keep the originals. Records created electronically (e-receipts, emailed invoices) must be kept electronically and be readable. Back up rewritable media and be able to restore in a CRA-usable format. The page does not address phone photos specifically. [S3, S4; IC05-1R1 dated 2010, rule on CRA permission to destroy conflicts across pages]
- **Practical consequence for RELIF:** keep the original (paper receipt or original e-receipt file) as well as the photo; store original bytes + SHA-256 hash + capture timestamp; log the capture (date, who) similar to the imaging logbook; offer "paper destroyed?" flag defaulting to "no".
- **What auditors ask for on rentals** (not found as an official CRA list; inferred from T4036 content): rent receipts/lease/bank deposits for income, invoices for repairs and capital items, property tax/insurance/utility bills, mortgage interest statement, basis for personal/rented % (floor plan, rooms), mileage log, closing statement land/building split. Ledger should map every entry to one of these.

## 3. The "is it deductible?" question flow (max 8 questions)
Mapping uses T776 lines from session 1 (config data, verify each year). T4036 current-vs-capital tests: lasting benefit? maintain or improve? part of property or separate asset? [S1]

1. **Which property/unit, and how many days/area is it rental?** (Property picker; rented area / total area or rooms; STR flag.) If the thing is for the owner's own unit only -> NOT DEDUCTIBLE (personal). If for a rented unit only -> 100%. If common/shared (roof, property tax, insurance, utilities) -> go on with the stored rental % (m2 or rooms; T4036 example 3 of 12 rooms = 25%). In a shared house: repairs only to rented rooms 100%; shared areas by availability/people sharing. [S1]
2. **Is the property available for rent / earning rent in this period?** (Before-first-rental: general expenses and CCA usually require the property to be available for use; soft costs of construction/renovation are special: current only to extent of rental income from the building, else capitalised.) Unsure -> FLAG. [S1]
3. **What is the purchase?** (pick type: repair/maintenance, appliance/furniture, improvement/renovation, utility, insurance, tax, professional fee, advertising, loan/mortgage payment, other.)
4. **If a loan payment: interest or principal?** Interest on money borrowed to buy/improve rental = deductible (line 8710); principal = NOT DEDUCTIBLE; loan fees deducted over 5 years; refinanced funds used personally -> that portion's interest not deductible. [S1]
5. **If repair/improvement/asset: current or capital?** Apply the T4036 tests. Restores to original condition = current (line 8960). Improves beyond original, or is a separate asset (fridge, stove) = capital -> CCA: Class 8 (20%) for furniture, appliances, tools >= $500 (cheaper tools are normally current in the guide's treatment; verify); Class 1 (4%) buildings after 1987; Class 6 fences; roof/landscaping not confirmed in my excerpt -> FLAG. Half-year rule in acquisition year; CCA cannot create or increase a rental loss; asset must be available for use. Land is never depreciable; for acquisitions, split fees by building share. [S1]
6. **Is any of it personal use or non-rental?** (Mixed purchase, e.g. hardware store trip, vehicle.) Ask % rental; apply to line 9949 personal portion. Vehicle -> needs mileage log (line 9281). Hired labour is deductible but the owner's own labour is not. [S1, S5]
7. **Is this an STR (<90 days) and is the unit compliant with local licensing for the nights involved?** Since 2024 expenses/CCA for non-compliant STR are denied (formula expense x non-compliant days / total STR days; lines 9365-9367; 2024 transitional relief). Non-compliant -> NOT DEDUCTIBLE or partial. [session 1]
8. **Is there a reasonable expectation of profit / below-market or cost-sharing rental to relatives?** If no -> expenses not claimable / no rental loss. Otherwise ok. [S1]

**Outcomes**
| Outcome | Trigger | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Deductible current expense | rental use 100%, current, compliant | T776 line (8521 advertising, 8690 insurance, 8710 interest/bank, 8810 office, 8860 professional, 8871 management, 8960 repairs, 9060 wages, 9180 property tax, 9200 travel, 9220 utilities, 9270 other, 9281 vehicle) |
| Capital -> CCA | fails current test | CCA class (8, 1, ...), capital register entry, half-year rule note |
| Partially deductible | shared/mixed use | rental % x amount; remainder to 9949 |
| Not deductible | personal unit, principal, land transfer tax (added to cost), penalties, own labour, non-compliant STR | reason + citation |
| Unsure | pre-rental period, GST/HST ambiguity, roof/landscaping, unusual | "Flag for accountant" with the question pre-filled |

Implement as a small decision tree in YAML + Python (deterministic, testable, cites a T4036 section). No LLM needed for verdict; optional LLM only to parse free text ("bought a fridge at Home Depot for $900") into slots.

## 4. Minimum fields per ledger entry (abridged ledger)
- id, captured_at (UTC timestamp), expense_date, amount_total, currency (CAD), gst_hst_amount (nullable), vendor, location/city (user's "location of purchase"), description (1 line), payment_method (card last4/cash/e-transfer), property_id (+ unit), allocation_pct_rental, deductible_amount, outcome (current/capital/partial/no/unsure), t776_line, cca_class (nullable), reason + rule_ref (e.g. T4036 "current vs capital"), user_answers (JSON of flow answers), receipt_file (path), receipt_sha256, receipt_kind (original e-receipt / photo of paper / none), paper_original_kept (bool), tax_year, retain_until (computed: 31 Dec tax_year+6; if late filed, filing date+6y; indefinite for capital/property acquisition docs), notes, accountant_flag.
- Abridged = one row per purchase, no bank import required; receipt file attached when available; "no receipt" is allowed but entry flagged high-risk.

## 6. Liability wording
- Not found: a source on how Canadian tax apps word it (searches didn't return it); the following is a design recommendation, not legal advice; get a lawyer/accountant to review before public release.
- Position: informational record-keeping aid, not tax advice; CRA's own text is the authority; user decides and is responsible.
- Verdict phrasing: use "Generally deductible under CRA guidance (T4036, 'Current vs capital expenses')" / "Likely capital: CCA Class 8 may apply" / "Not generally deductible: principal repayments" / "Unsure: ask an accountant". Avoid "you can claim" / "approved". Every answer shows the citation and the assumptions entered ("assuming rented area is 25%").
- Footer on every export: "Prepared from your own entries. Not tax advice. Does not file with or send to CRA. Confirm with CRA or a tax professional."
- Never file, never submit, never store others' data, no hosted service in v1 (reduces privacy and liability surface).
- Keep rules versioned by tax year with the citation URL so wrong answers can be traced and fixed.

## 7. Capture channel + storage + yearly export bundle
- Channels (solo Python builder), ranked:
  1. **Telegram bot** (python-telegram-bot): fits the mobile moment (photo + "$900 fridge Home Depot rental unit"); user already has Telegram tooling in this repo's ecosystem. Caveat: receipts transit Telegram servers (not private end-to-end for bots) -> conflicts with "private" claim; mitigate by running the bot on user's machine, deleting messages optional, or stating the tradeoff plainly.
  2. **Local web form** (FastAPI/Flask or NiceGUI on localhost / home LAN or Tailscale): private, phone camera upload via browser; needs HTML (ask user first per repo rules).
  3. **CLI** (`relif ask`): simplest, good for the first prototype and tests; poor at the store aisle.
- Storage: SQLite (ledger) + `receipts/YYYY/<id>.<ext>` files, hashes in DB; encrypted backup via `age` or zip-with-AES to user-chosen drive; remember CRA wants records in Canada (do not default to a foreign cloud). Restore test documented (CRA expects restorable backups).
- Yearly export bundle `relif-2026/`: `ledger.csv` (all fields), `t776_summary.csv` (sum by line per property), `capital_register.csv` (CCA class, cost, half-year), `unsure.csv` (flagged), `receipts/` (original files named date_vendor_amount), `MANIFEST.sha256`, `README.txt` (retain-until dates, disclaimer), optional PDF of the ledger. A separate `audit_request` export: filter by line/date range + receipts, one folder.
- Fills T776 at filing: user keys the line totals into their tax software; RELIF never files.

## 9. Risks / riskiest assumption now
- **Riskiest assumption:** owners will ask RELIF at purchase time and log each expense (habit + trust), and a rule-based verdict from a free tool is accurate enough for them to rely on. Cheapest test: 5 landlords log 2 weeks of real purchases via a Telegram/CLI prototype; count entries/week and wrong verdicts. Alternative test: give them 10 realistic purchases and compare the flow's outcome vs an accountant's.
- Verdict accuracy: edge cases (pre-rental period, roofs/landscaping, GST/HST, shared house) -> "unsure" must be a first-class outcome.
- Evidence quality: phone photos may not meet CGSB-72.34; mitigate by keeping originals and saying so.
- Privacy claim vs Telegram transit; cloud backups outside Canada.
- Yearly maintenance of lines/classes; scope creep (GST/HST ITCs, partnerships, foreign property).
- Differentiation vs $9-10/mo Canadian apps already with receipt capture.
