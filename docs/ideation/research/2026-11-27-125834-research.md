# RELIF - Rental Ledger for Income-tax Filing: level-1 research (2026-11-27)

## 1. Problem & who has it badly
- Small/accidental landlords (1-5 units): basement-suite owners, people renting a room, "moved out, kept the house" owners, co-owning spouses/siblings. They pay personal and rental costs from the same accounts and only reconstruct T776 numbers in March/April.
- Pain points: splitting shared costs (property tax, insurance, utilities, mortgage interest) between personal and rented portions; current vs capital (a new fridge is capital, repairing original brickwork is current); mortgage interest vs principal; CCA/half-year rule; co-owner shares; 6-year record keeping.
- Mismatch of tools: tax software (Wealthsimple Tax, TurboTax, UFile, H&R Block) takes T776 totals as input but does NOT categorise a year of bank transactions. Landlord suites are US-centric or pitched at larger portfolios.

### T1 lines
- 12599 Gross rental income = total gross rents on T776. Co-owners report GROSS income for the whole property in the guide's income-lines text, but net income/loss by ownership share (the guide's co-ownership text: report share of income or loss per ownership). Needs clarification with the CRA page before coding: store both "property total" and "my share".
- 12600 Net rental income = after expenses. Loss goes in brackets.
- Partners with a T5013 slip fill only partnership BN, % and box 110/107 amount.

### Rules that change the numbers
- Renting part of your home: claim 100% of costs of the rented area plus proportional share (by m2 or rooms) of whole-property costs. No deduction if no reasonable expectation of profit.
- Renting below market / cost-sharing with family: no rental loss.
- Not deductible: land transfer tax (add to cost), mortgage principal, penalties, value of own labour.
- Short-term rentals (<90 consecutive days, residential): since 1 Jan 2024, expenses and CCA for NON-COMPLIANT STRs (not meeting local registration/licensing/permit rules) are denied. Formula per property: expense x non-compliant days / total STR days. Transitional: if compliant by 31 Dec 2024, whole 2024 treated compliant (2024 only). Commentary says no reassessment time limit (Lexpert, Bennett Jones). Municipal bylaws can be stricter.
- Change in use (principal residence <-> rental) = deemed sale at FMV and reacquisition; s.45(2) election lets you skip it but no CCA, and principal-residence designation up to 4 years. Reported on Schedule 3 / T2091(IND). Flipping rule: housing unit owned <365 days -> business income.
- Records: keep receipts, invoices, contracts 6 years after the tax year; do not send with return.
- Proposed/new CCA incentives (purpose-built rentals 10% rate, Class 50 etc.) exist in 2025/26 guide excerpts; mostly irrelevant to 1-5 unit landlords but CCA class table must be versioned.

### Extra questions RELIF should ask the user (guided interview)
1. Which properties; ownership % and co-owners; do you hold a T5013 (partnership)?
2. Whole property rented, or part of home? Rented area vs total (m2 or rooms) -> personal-use %.
3. Principal residence ever / change in use this year? Elected s.45(2)? Sold this year (recapture, Schedule 3)?
4. Short-term rental (<90 days)? Registered/licensed in your municipality for every night? Non-compliant days count.
5. Rent charged at fair market value? Any tenants who are relatives (cost-sharing)?
6. Did you buy appliances/furniture/improvements (capital, CCA class)? Claim CCA or not (CCA optional, affects future recapture and principal residence)?
7. Mortgage: interest only? Refinanced and used funds personally? Loan fees paid?
8. Vehicle use for rental and mileage log; hired help (T4/T4A obligations); GST/HST status (not verified here).
9. Foreign rental property (T1135 if >C$100k cost) - not verified in the T4036 excerpt; check separately. Out of scope for MVP: warn and stop.
10. Rental losses: reasonable expectation of profit?

## 5. Product shape & smallest MVP
Principles: Python-first, local-first, free, prepares numbers, does not file.

MVP (a few weekends):
1. Python CLI (or Streamlit/NiceGUI local app): `relif import bank.csv` for Canadian bank/credit-card CSVs (RBC, TD, BMO, Scotia, CIBC, Tangerine, Wealthsimple presets; generic column mapper).
2. Rules engine (YAML): payee/regex -> {personal, rental property X, T776 bucket}; learned from user confirmations. Split rules for shared costs (property tax x rented %). Optional later: tiny local classifier (scikit-learn) for suggestions only.
3. Mortgage statement helper: separate interest vs principal (user enters annual interest from lender statement).
4. Capital item register with CCA class + half-year rule.
5. Year-end report: T776 line table (per property, per owner share) + T1 12599/12600 figures + a checklist of the extra questions + audit list of uncategorised items. Export CSV/PDF; attach receipt files into a per-year folder with hashes; 6-year retention reminder.
6. Config files for line numbers and CCA classes per tax year (versioned data, so CRA changes do not need code changes).

Standalone vs lease module: recommendation = **standalone core library + CLI, shipped with a thin "RELIF" tab/export bridge to lease/Rent Roll**, not built inside the single HTML file.
Reasons:
- lease is a single-file browser/localStorage app; bank-CSV parsing, rules, receipts, and 6-year retention are heavy for localStorage (5MB quota, no file store, no backups) and the user's Python strength does not apply.
- Audiences overlap but jobs differ: lease = tenancy workflow (screen, lease, notices), Rent Roll = rent/utility receipts (income side), RELIF = expense categorisation and tax.
- Income side is shared: Rent Roll's rent records should feed line 8141 -> import via JSON/CSV; keep a shared property/tenant schema.
- Standalone keeps tax-disclaimer risk separate from the tenancy toolkit's brand.
- Option: later add a browser-only (Pyodide/JS) port if landlords want zero-install; start local Python.

1. Free and open source (fits user values); builds audience for lease toolkit.
2. Donations/GitHub Sponsors; low yield.
3. One-time paid "year pack" (e.g. $15-29) with bank presets and PDF report, sold via Gumroad; undercuts $6.99-$39/month SaaS.

Realistic expectation: low revenue; value is portfolio/audience and solving own problem.

## 7. Risks and the riskiest assumption
- Riskiest assumption: small landlords will import bank CSVs and maintain categories year-round, and will trust a free hobby tool over tax software they already pay for. Cheaper to test: ask 5-10 landlords to categorise last year's CSV with a prototype in one sitting.
- Tax-advice risk: must present as record-keeping/estimation, not advice; "verify with CRA/accountant"; never file or submit.
- Correctness risk: wrong line numbers/CCA logic harm users; CRA forms change yearly (new columns, STR charts) -> yearly maintenance burden.
- Scope creep: GST/HST, partnerships, foreign property, business vs rental income, non-resident (Part XIII/s.216) - exclude and detect via questions.
- Co-owner ambiguity: gross vs share handling for 12599 must be confirmed (see section 2).
- Competition: tax software could add transaction import; Wealthsimple/others bundle with filing.
- Data: bank CSV formats vary and change; categorisation accuracy of personal vs rental for shared accounts is inherently ambiguous -> rules plus confirm UI, not full automation.
